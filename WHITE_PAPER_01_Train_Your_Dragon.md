**A live-tested working model for integrating existing AI into personal life under human ownership**

---

## Abstract

Most AI interaction resets context, concentrates durable state in the platform, and treats the human as a temporary user. Train Your Dragon is a live-tested alternative: the human builds and owns durable external systems (structured memory, skills, process rules) so existing AI capabilities compound under human direction.

This paper documents the working model, its principles, development history, and relation to digital minimalism and AI-second-brain systems. It does not claim to solve global AI governance. It claims that **one human can keep agency** in daily AI collaboration by design — and that this personal node is the necessary building block for any larger network that still respects ownership.

---

## 1. Problem: Collaboration Without Ownership

Default AI use fails the individual in three ways:

1. **Ephemeral context** — Progress does not compound across sessions.
2. **Blurred ownership** — The platform holds the only durable state.
3. **Values from above** — Institutional safety layers are real and useful, but the individual cannot version or revise the value layer that governs *their* daily work.

Agency is partially transferred by default. The tool is strong in the moment and weak across time for the person using it.

---

## 2. Agency as Non-Negotiable

**Agency** means the human decides what is remembered, discarded, skilled, ruled, and revised.

Requirements for any AI interaction under this model:

- AI is **builder and thinking partner**, not owner or trainer.
- Durable state lives in **external systems the human controls**.
- Rules are **versioned** (review on roadblock, ~6 months, or model change).
- **Personal** material and **method** material stay separated.

Without these, collaboration drifts into dependency.

---

## 3. Why Organization Is Required

Agency needs a place for things to land. Without it: capture fails, context resets, ownership blurs.

Train Your Dragon uses activity-based structure (PARA-style: Projects, Areas, Resources, Archives) for personal material, plus a public method layer for capability evolution. PARA was adopted as an existing method, not reinvented. The contribution is applying it to **human–AI collaboration under explicit ownership**.

Habits that matter: capture first, file second, review on friction.

---

## 4. Working Model

### Operating pattern

1. Human trains their own dragon.
2. Structured memory file is the core.
3. Skills serve the core; they do not replace it.
4. Prefer durable external systems over chat history.
5. Iterate: use → friction → smallest useful improvement.
6. Version deliberately.
7. When work improves *how* collaboration runs, record it as method so future sessions inherit it.

### Two kinds of work

| Kind | Home | Examples |
|------|------|----------|
| Personal | Private PARA / memory | Life, health, course notes, self-reflection |
| Method | Public Train-Your-Dragon-AI | Bootloader, capture rules, process review, ownership principles |

### Live-tested elements (2026)

- Versioned structured memory
- Public principles under human review before push
- Bootloader / cross-instance orientation
- Collaboration & clarity protocol (beta)
- Capture inbox pattern
- Course and development material outside chat-only storage

## 6. Related Work and Digital Minimalism

**AI second brain / PARA + AI** systems optimize continuity and personal context. Overlap: durable memory, integration. Gap: fewer treat **ownership**, **versioning of the human’s rules**, and **personal vs method** as first-class.

**Digital minimalism** defends attention and intentional tool use (clutter is costly; optimize how tools are used; intentionality satisfies). Overlap: agency over attention. Difference: minimalism often answers *use less*; Train Your Dragon answers *integrate under ownership* so capability compounds without transferring the center of decision.

---

## 7. Values Layer: Institutional Safety vs Personal Agency

Institutional safety training has value and cost. It sets baselines; it does not automatically give the individual a revisable value layer for daily collaboration.

True personal agency sits in what the human allows into durable systems, what they version, and whether the AI remains partner or becomes owner of continuity. Train Your Dragon places that layer **outside the model weights**, under human control.

---

## 8. Position on General AI

This working model does not require AGI. Continuity, personal values, and specialized direction are already approachable by marrying current models with human-owned durable systems. The author’s position: **meaningful compounding collaboration does not wait on general AI**; it waits on humans who train and own their systems.

A single general agent that holds all context increases agency risk. Many owned personal nodes reduce it.

---

## 9. Role in a Larger Network

Train Your Dragon is designed as a **personal node**: private core, publishable method-only surface. That node is the unit assumed by the companion paper on decentralized networked intelligence under human agency.

This paper stops at the working personal system. The network architecture is specified separately so the personal model can be adopted without accepting any particular global design.

---

## 10. Conclusion

Train Your Dragon is a live-tested pattern for agency-first AI collaboration: durable external systems, human ownership, versioned rules, personal/method separation.

It is one human’s answer to ephemeral context and blurred ownership. It is also the intended building block for any network that claims to keep human agency at the core.
